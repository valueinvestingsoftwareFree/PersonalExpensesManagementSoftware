How Do I Prepare a Yearly Financial Review?

A yearly financial review is one of the most valuable activities you can perform to improve your financial future. While monthly reviews help you stay organized, an annual review gives you the opportunity to evaluate your overall progress, measure long-term goals, and prepare a better financial strategy for the year ahead.

Instead of focusing only on individual transactions, a yearly review allows you to analyze trends, identify strengths and weaknesses, evaluate investment performance, and determine whether your financial decisions are helping you build lasting wealth.

Why Perform a Yearly Financial Review?

Many people work hard throughout the year but never stop to evaluate their overall financial progress. Without an annual review, it becomes difficult to know whether your income increased, your expenses improved, your savings grew, or your investments performed as expected.

A yearly financial review provides perspective. It transforms hundreds or thousands of individual transactions into meaningful financial information that can guide better decisions for the future.

Benefits of an Annual Financial Review

  • Measure yearly financial progress.
  • Review income growth.
  • Analyze annual expenses.
  • Evaluate savings performance.
  • Review debt reduction.
  • Measure investment returns.
  • Calculate annual net worth growth.
  • Create better financial goals for next year.

Step 1: Review Your Annual Income

Begin by calculating your total income for the year. Include salaries, bonuses, freelance work, business income, rental income, dividends, interest, and every additional source of revenue.

Comparing your annual income with previous years allows you to measure career growth, business performance, and the success of additional income sources.

Step 2: Analyze Your Annual Spending

Review how your money was spent throughout the year. Categorizing annual expenses helps identify spending habits that may not be obvious during monthly reviews.

Category Annual Review
Housing Rent, mortgage, utilities
Transportation Fuel, insurance, maintenance
Food Groceries and restaurants
Healthcare Medical expenses and insurance
Savings & Investments Total yearly contributions

Focus on Long-Term Trends

The objective of a yearly review is not to criticize individual purchases. Instead, it is to identify long-term spending patterns that can help improve future financial decisions.

Step 3: Compare Your Budget With Actual Results

Review your annual budget and compare it with what actually happened during the year. Understanding these differences helps you build more realistic budgets and improve financial planning for the coming year.
