How Do I Calculate My Net Worth?

Your net worth is one of the most important indicators of your overall financial health. Unlike simply looking at your bank account balance, your net worth shows the complete picture of what you own compared with what you owe. It is a powerful measurement that helps you evaluate financial progress, monitor wealth growth, and make better long-term financial decisions.

Whether you are just beginning your financial journey or have been investing for years, calculating your net worth regularly allows you to understand how your financial position changes over time. Even small monthly improvements can lead to significant long-term financial success.

What Is Net Worth?

Net worth is calculated by subtracting your total liabilities from your total assets.

The Formula Is Simple

Net Worth = Total Assets − Total Liabilities

If your assets are greater than your liabilities, your net worth is positive. If your liabilities exceed your assets, your net worth is negative. The goal is to steadily increase your net worth throughout your lifetime by growing assets while reducing debt.

What Counts as an Asset?

Assets are everything you own that has financial value. Depending on your situation, assets may include:

  • Checking and savings accounts.
  • Cash.
  • Stocks and ETFs.
  • Mutual funds.
  • Retirement accounts.
  • Investment portfolios.
  • Real estate.
  • Vehicles.
  • Business ownership.
  • Valuable personal property.

Including all major assets provides a more accurate picture of your total wealth.

What Counts as a Liability?

Liabilities represent everything you owe to other people or financial institutions. Common liabilities include mortgages, personal loans, student loans, vehicle loans, credit card balances, lines of credit, and any other outstanding debt.

Reducing liabilities is one of the fastest ways to improve your overall net worth because every payment decreases the amount you owe while increasing your financial stability.

Track Net Worth Over Time

Your net worth should not be calculated only once. Measuring it every month or every quarter allows you to identify trends, evaluate financial decisions, and stay motivated as your assets continue growing and your debts gradually decline.

Why Net Worth Matters

Many people focus only on income, but income alone does not determine financial success. Someone with a high salary and significant debt may have a lower net worth than someone earning less but consistently saving, investing, and living within their means. Monitoring net worth gives you a much clearer picture of your long-term financial progress than income alone.
