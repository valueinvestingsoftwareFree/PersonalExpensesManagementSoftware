Does Exporting Financial Data Mean Exporting the Entire Database?

Not necessarily. There are several different meanings of “exporting financial data.”

Some users want a copy of individual transactions. Others want reports that can be opened in spreadsheet software. Some want a complete database backup so they can restore the entire financial environment later.

These are different purposes and should not be confused.

For example, a spreadsheet containing transactions may be useful for analysis, but it may not contain all the relationships, settings or structures required to recreate an entire financial application database.

A complete database backup can therefore be much more useful when the goal is disaster recovery or preserving the complete application data.

Export vs. Backup: What Is the Difference?

Exporting and backing up financial information are related, but they are not the same thing.

An export generally converts selected information into a format that can be used somewhere else. A backup is primarily intended to preserve information so that it can be restored after data loss or a system problem.

Think of export as portability and backup as protection.

Both are important. Exporting helps you move or analyze your information, while backups help you recover it if something goes wrong.

Can I Keep a Backup of My Financial Database?

Yes. Maintaining backups is one of the most important things you can do when using software that stores important financial information locally.

A local database provides control, but that control also means that you should take responsibility for protecting your data against hardware failure, accidental deletion, malware, corruption or other unexpected problems.

A good backup strategy should ideally include more than one copy of your important financial information. Keeping backup copies on separate storage can reduce the risk that a single hardware problem destroys both the primary database and its backup.

  • Create regular backups of important financial data.
  • Keep backup copies separate from the primary computer.
  • Protect backup storage from unauthorized access.
  • Keep more than one backup when the information is particularly important.
  • Periodically verify that your backups can actually be restored.

A Simple Financial Data Portability Strategy

You do not need a complicated strategy to begin protecting your financial information. A consistent process is more important than complexity.

  1. Keep your financial records organized. Use consistent categories and maintain accurate transaction information.
  2. Maintain your primary database. Keep your financial information in a structured system rather than relying exclusively on scattered files.
  3. Create regular backups. Preserve copies of your financial database so that a hardware or software problem does not destroy your history.
  4. Keep backup copies separately. A backup stored on the same physical device as the original may not protect you from every type of failure.
  5. Understand your export options. Know how your financial information can be moved or analyzed outside your primary application.
  6. Test restoration periodically. A backup is most valuable when you know that it can be successfully restored when necessary.
